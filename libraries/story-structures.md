# Story Structures

A body without an arc rambles. Pick one structure, outline the beats, then write. The structure is the skeleton. The voice is the skin.

## The structures

### Problem, shift, proof
1. Name the problem the reader feels.
2. Shift to the truth they have not considered.
3. Prove it with a specific number, name, or result.

Use when teaching. Best for the Education pillar where the reader needs to understand something they currently have wrong.

### Before and after
1. Show the before state honestly. Do not soften it.
2. Show what changed and the one thing that drove it.
3. Show the after, with proof attached.

Use for transformation and results. Best for the Proof pillar where the brand has a result to show, not just a lesson to give.

### Myth correction
1. State the myth the audience believes. Name it plainly.
2. Show why it is wrong, with evidence or reasoning.
3. Replace it with the simpler truth and what to do instead.

Use to debunk hype or popular bad advice. Best for the Education pillar when the audience has been misled by something the brand can disprove.

### List with a turn
1. Open with a tight, specific list.
2. Walk the items fast. One line each.
3. Turn at the end: the one that actually mattered, or the one to skip entirely.

Use for practical, scannable posts where the reader wants information fast and the turn adds the non-obvious insight.

### Behind the curtain
1. Open on a real moment from the work. Specific place, specific time, specific situation.
2. Show the decision or mistake inside it. What was on the table.
3. Land on what it taught, in one line. The lesson is the ending.

Use for founder and process posts. Best for the Behind the studio pillar where the work itself is the content and the reader wants access they could not get anywhere else.

## How to use

1. Read the locked pillar, angle, and hook from the Brick chain.
2. Pick the arc that fits the pillar and keeps the hook's promise.
3. Outline three or four beats in one line each. Lock the outline.
4. Write the body to the outline. One idea only. End on the turn, not a recap.

## Self-test

- Does each beat earn the next, or could you remove it without losing anything?
- Is there exactly one idea, or did a second idea sneak in?
- Does the ending land a point, or does it just stop?

## Rules

- One idea per post. Never two.
- No em dashes or hyphens used as sentence breaks. No emojis.
- Show, do not claim. Every claim needs a number, a name, or a result. No invented stats.
