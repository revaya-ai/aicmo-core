# Hook Library

Line one decides whether anyone reads line two. A hook is not a summary. It opens a gap the reader has to close. Pick one pattern, fill it with the real topic, and keep it to five to ten words.

## The patterns

### Information gap
State that something is true and withhold the reason. The reader has to keep reading to get it.
- Shape: "The product that sold out was the one we almost killed."
- Why it works: the reader already wants to know why. You just named the gap.

### Contrarian
Say the opposite of the common advice, then back it up in the body.
- Shape: "A longer routine made my skin worse."
- Why it works: the reader has followed the common advice. You just broke their assumption.

### Number
Lead with a specific, surprising count. Specific beats round.
- Shape: "I tested 14 serums. Three were worth it."
- Why it works: a specific number signals there is real information behind the post.

### Question as statement
Ask a question the reader is already asking, in their words.
- Shape: "Why does your skin look worse after a week off?"
- Why it works: they have asked this exact question. You are the one answering it.

### Callout
Name the exact reader and the exact moment.
- Shape: "If you own a small brand and cannot afford a CMO, read this."
- Why it works: the reader feels seen immediately. Nobody else is writing to them.

### Confession
Admit a mistake. Honesty earns the next line.
- Shape: "We shipped a product that did not work. Here is what it cost."
- Why it works: it breaks the expectation of brand polish. The reader trusts it.

### Pattern interrupt
Break the expected frame in the first three words.
- Shape: "Stop buying the serum."
- Why it works: the reader was about to scroll. The unexpected stop lands before they decide.

## How to choose

1. Read the locked angle from the Angle step.
2. Pick the pattern that creates the most real tension for this audience and pillar.
3. Fill it with the actual topic. No placeholder language.
4. Cut it to five to ten words. If it needs a comma to breathe, it is too long.

## Self-test

- Does it open a gap, or does it summarize? Summaries lose.
- Would the reader feel the need to read line two?
- Is it specific to this topic, or could it sit on any post?

## Rules

- Five to ten words.
- No em dashes or hyphens as sentence breaks. No emojis.
- No throat-clearing. Never open with "in today's world" or any form of scene-setting.
