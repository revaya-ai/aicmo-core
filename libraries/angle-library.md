# Angle Library

A topic is not a post. The angle is the specific take that makes a topic land for a specific reader. Load this library at the Angle step, after the topic and pillar are locked, and before the hook is written.

## What an angle is

The angle answers: "Of all the ways to say this, which one creates tension this reader feels?" It is chosen against the client audience and the pillar already locked in the topic step.

## Named angle library

Pick one. Each is reusable across topics.

- **The Simpler Truth**: the honest, less complicated version of a thing people overcomplicate. Use when the audience is overwhelmed by conflicting advice.
- **The Hidden Cost**: the real price of the status quo the reader has not counted. Use when the reader is stuck doing nothing because they do not see what inaction costs.
- **The Myth Correction**: name a common belief, show why it is wrong, give the fix. Use when the audience has been misled by popular advice.
- **The Insider View**: what people who actually do this know that outsiders do not. Use when the brand has earned-knowledge no one else in the market can match.
- **The Contrarian Take**: the defensible opposite of the popular advice. Use when the popular advice is provably wrong for this audience.
- **The Origin Lesson**: a specific moment or mistake that taught the lesson, told plainly. Use for founder and process content where the lesson is the product.
- **The Cost Of Doing Nothing**: what keeps getting worse while the reader waits. Use to break inertia when the reader knows they need to act but is delaying.
- **The Permission Slip**: telling the reader they are allowed to stop doing the exhausting thing. Use when the audience is over-functioning and blaming themselves for poor results.
- **The Quiet Win**: the small, unglamorous habit that beats the flashy one. Use when the audience is chasing quick fixes and missing the boring thing that actually works.
- **The Comparison**: two paths side by side, with the honest tradeoff named. Use when the reader is deciding between two options and needs clarity, not hype.

## How to choose

1. Read the primary audience and what turns them off from the brand-and-audience file.
2. Find the tension between what they want and what they have been told.
3. Pick the angle that names that tension most directly for the locked pillar.
4. If two angles fit, pick the one the brand can back with a specific fact, number, or result.

## Output format

Return exactly two lines:

- Angle: one named angle from the library above
- Why: one sentence on why it fits this audience and pillar
